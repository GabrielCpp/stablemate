A rule above any one service lives in a page of the product book (`products/<product>/<slug>.md` in the artifacts folder, where the product is `book.product` in the repository's `agents.yml` and else the repository's name; a repository with `book.home: repo` keeps it as `product/<slug>.md` beside `services/`), the team book (`teams/<team>/<slug>.md` in the artifacts dir) or the company book (`company/<slug>.md` in the artifacts dir), and the coder's turns read them before they judge or write. The spec turn and the dev turns run `ostler book pages` and read every page it lists, because they cannot list the team and company folders by path, and they use its words. The book turn, the end review and the resolver run `ostler book search` once for each main noun of the story with `--kind company`, `--kind team`, `--kind product` and `--kind decision`. The order of authority is company, team, product, service decision, story. A criterion that contradicts a page is reported as such, never silently judged, and a conflict between two levels is reported and never settled by the agent.

The operator's resolver treats the pages and the service decisions as the only standing decisions, ahead of the story. No other store of decisions exists, and no turn writes one outside a product page or a service decision. A question a page settles is not escalated and is not decided against the page. When the operator's answer states a rule that outlives the story, the resolver writes it as a product page in the same turn, in the product folder, with `sources: operator` and the date. The resolver never writes a team or a company page. When the answer reads as a rule for more than this product, the sources line also says `may belong to the team book`, so a person can move the page. An answer that explains one element becomes a line in that service's decision.
