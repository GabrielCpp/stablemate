# okf_book catch_up

The catch-up flow, which brings each service book up to the app's HEAD from its stale report.

`flow.py` holds the machine, `nodes.py` the fill, the diff and the records of each book, `report.py` the reading of `ostler book stale`, `register.py` the register of declined notes, `batches.py` the cutting of a book's notes into batches, `lines.py` the lines the run ends on, and `prompts/update-book.md` the owner's turn. The gates are the nodes of `shared/book_gates.py`, which `python_book` calls too. The operator gates, their questions and the lap cap are in `shared/book_flow.py`, and both flows hand the operator's answer to the owner's next turn through the same method.

A book is filed when its stale report names anything, or when it names nothing and `ostler book check` is red in one of its modules. The check is the tool's own, so a user who installed the tool has it. A book filed for the red check alone is filed for the format: its findings, each naming the element and what to state, are the owner's work list, and the commit says so in its subject. `fill` runs the check of each module of every filed book, stale or not, and `BookReport.for_format` is the one place that decides: a check that is red or printed a note the register does not cover. The notes are a second work list. `report.py` groups them by kind, the text after the element's name and its colon cut at the next colon or at 60 characters. The owner states what a note asks or declines it in `CatchUpReply.declined`, `<Element>: <reason>` or `<kind>: <reason>`, and `catch_up_declined` writes those lines into the register `services/<name>/DECLINED.md`, `- <element>: <kind>: <reason>` or `- *: <kind>: <reason>`, which the batch commit carries. A note the register covers is no work: it is not listed, not counted as remaining and counted as declined in the outcome lines. A green book whose notes the register covers is current. The before numbers of the move are recorded when the book is taken, so no log line shows a before of 0.

A book for the format is worked in batches. `begin` opens one: `plan_batch` takes every blocking finding of the check and the next `batch_notes` uncovered notes, kind by kind in the order the check printed them, and `catch_up_batch` keeps the open batch and the committed ones under `batches/<service>.json` in the run folder. The prompt lists only that batch and says its size. The laps, the check, the commit checks, the play and the stamp run on the batch as before, and a listed note neither gone nor declined after a passing check sends the owner another lap on the same batch, under `MAX_LAPS`, and at the cap waits on the operator gate. `commit` commits the batch and `batch_outcome` counts it: `asked`, `done`, `outside` (notes not listed that the check no longer prints), `laps` and `minutes`. The minutes are the `minutes` of the owner turn that `_timed_turn` records in `metrics.jsonl`, the wall clock around the agent call. With notes left and fewer than `batches` batches done, `commit` goes on to `begin`, and otherwise the book is settled as `moved, N notes remain`. The parameters are `batch_notes` (15), `batches` (1) and `play_minutes` (15). `python_book_play` runs a play under `play_minutes` with `run_tool(..., timeout=...)`, and a play past it exits 3 with `the play did not end within <N> minutes (play_minutes)`, which `_judged` sends to the environment gate. A resumed or new run reads the check again, so no batch number is stored in the book.

For an operator, one run is one batch of work per book. Run `catch_up` again for the next batch, or set `batches` for more in one run. The last lines are one per batch, one per kind of note and `<n> notes remain: run catch_up again, about <k> batches`, for example `web: batch 1: 15 asked, 15 done, 0 outside, 1 lap, 14 min, played 6 calls and 2 journeys green, committed`.

`shared/book_format.py` reads the history of the `ostler-book` skill from a stablemate checkout. That history is optional context: with it the owner also gets the commits and their diff, and `FORMAT` is written at the head of the history after the gates pass. Without a checkout, or when the history cannot be read, the run goes on, logs that the format history was not available, and writes no `FORMAT`. No operator gate waits on it.

`play` never plays a whole module. For a book filed for the format, `python_book_affected` runs `ostler book affected` with `--since` at the app's HEAD, so the diff is the owner's uncommitted edits, and `play` runs `--only` on the plan's calls and `--partial --journey` on its journeys, `--fresh-data` on the first play of a module. A whole plan plays what it names and records its reason as owed. A scheduled `ostler book run <module>` is where a whole play belongs. `catch_up_move` keeps the numbers of that play and the check after the last lap, with each kind of note as filed, left and declined. `summarize` logs the outcome lines last, one record per line: for a moved book one per batch, one per kind of note, the owed whole play and what remains. The commit message of a batch is its subject, with ` (batch N)` after the first when it fits, and a body of the batch's line and the per-kind lines.

A book whose service declares no app has no verified commit by construction: `ostler book stale` prints `<name> declares no app` and exits 2, and `report.py` reads that exact line as a first book, with no operator gate.
