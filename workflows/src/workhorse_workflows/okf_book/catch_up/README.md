# okf_book catch_up

The catch-up flow, which brings each service book up to the app's HEAD from its stale report.

`flow.py` holds the machine, `nodes.py` the fill, the diff and the records of each book, `report.py` the reading of `ostler book stale`, and `prompts/update-book.md` the owner's turn. The gates are the nodes of `shared/book_gates.py`, which `python_book` calls too. The operator gates, their questions and the lap cap are in `shared/book_flow.py`, and both flows hand the operator's answer to the owner's next turn through the same method.

A book is filed when its stale report names anything, or when it names nothing and `ostler book check` is red in one of its modules. The check is the tool's own, so a user who installed the tool has it. A book filed for the red check alone is filed for the format: its findings, each naming the element and what to state, are the owner's work list, and the commit says so in its subject. `fill` runs the check of each module of every filed book, stale or not, and `BookReport.for_format` is the one place that decides: a check that is red or printed notes. The notes are a second work list. `report.py` groups them by kind, the text after the element's name and its colon cut at the next colon or at 60 characters, and the prompt lists each kind with its count and up to 80 elements. The owner states what a note asks or declines it in `CatchUpReply.declined`, `<Element>: <reason>` or `<kind>: <reason>`, kept by `catch_up_declined` in `declined-notes/<service>.json` and on the book's outcome. After a passing check, a note of a kind the book was filed with that is neither gone nor declined sends the book another lap with the remaining notes, under `MAX_LAPS`, and at the cap the remaining notes wait on the operator gate. The before numbers of the move are recorded when the book is taken, so no log line shows a before of 0.

`shared/book_format.py` reads the history of the `ostler-book` skill from a stablemate checkout. That history is optional context: with it the owner also gets the commits and their diff, and `FORMAT` is written at the head of the history after the gates pass. Without a checkout, or when the history cannot be read, the run goes on, logs that the format history was not available, and writes no `FORMAT`. No operator gate waits on it.

`play` never plays a whole module. For a book filed for the format, `python_book_affected` runs `ostler book affected` with `--since` at the app's HEAD, so the diff is the owner's uncommitted edits, and `play` runs `--only` on the plan's calls and `--partial --journey` on its journeys, `--fresh-data` on the first play of a module. A whole plan plays what it names and records its reason as owed. A scheduled `ostler book run <module>` is where a whole play belongs. `catch_up_move` keeps the numbers of that play and the check after the last lap, with each kind of note as filed, left and declined. `summarize` logs the outcome lines last, one record per line: the book's line, one line per kind of note, and the owed whole play. The commit message of a moved book is its subject and a body of the per-kind lines.

A book whose service declares no app has no verified commit by construction: `ostler book stale` prints `<name> declares no app` and exits 2, and `report.py` reads that exact line as a first book, with no operator gate.
