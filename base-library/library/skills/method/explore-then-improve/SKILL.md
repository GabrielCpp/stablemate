# Explore, then improve

This is the **explore** state of a [[research-study]]. The study hands over its question,
its premises, its two bars, its `TRIED.md` and the budget share left. This skill hands back
one of two things: a **prototype** that cleared the outcome bar once, with its **ideal
path** priced for the study's ceiling test, or the sentence that every surviving framing
died, each for its own reason. The choice of mechanism is settled here by searching a field
of readings of the question, never by fixing a stage: a stage is fixed in [[self-improve]],
and the study arrives here because no stage was the problem.

The search is paperwork, and it is held to the study's sentence: it earns its time by
killing a candidate more cheaply than a run would. When a short run on things that exist
today would read the fact a card is arguing about, run it and cite the reading. A second
checker round on the same cards with no probe between them is a reflection trigger.

Every probe this skill runs, a short run that reads a fact or the prototype's own run,
carries the study's **probe gate** in its ledger row before it starts: the **ideal path** of
the mechanism it serves, or the sentence that the mechanism has none yet and the probe is
reading a term for it; the **roof** of the work it does, the fastest the hardware or the
task allows; and the **trace**, the row or record of a run already paid for that answers
the probe's question, or why none does. A probe whose trace names a row is cited, not run.

## 1. Frame the question

A **framing** is a reading of the question: what the thing under study does, on which
input, to produce which output, judged how, and what the question's key verb means. It
names no mechanism and no file. Write at least three, each built from its own choice on
every open axis, by the four moves in [references/framings.md](references/framings.md):
restate the question by analogy from distant fields, list the axes as fixed or open, draw
rows that differ on at least half of the open axes, and write each row in isolation. Merge
two framings that share input, output and role. Record them in `FRAMINGS.md` in the study's
working directory, in plain words, each with its status.

Done when `FRAMINGS.md` holds three or more framings that differ on half the open axes,
each with its status and the premise it reads from.

## 2. Map the field

Read `TRIED.md` and the project's own history first, then load [[brainstorm]] and
generate before judging. Give every surviving framing at least three candidates, at least
ten in all that differ in mechanism and not in size, at least three absent from `TRIED.md`,
at least two borrowed from another discipline, and one expected to fail with a sentence on
why it might not. The field always holds the two controls under "Controls" in the study's
measurement reference. For each candidate write its expected gain tied to a line of the
profile or of the bar, its cost to test, and the result that would kill it. Write the field
into `FIELD.md`, ordered by expected gain over cost to test.

Done when `FIELD.md` meets every count above and each candidate carries its gain, its cost
to test and its kill result.

## 3. Gate each candidate on paper

Before any build, write the candidate's card and pass it through the two gates in
[references/paper-gates.md](references/paper-gates.md). Gate 1 asks whether it answers the
question as posed: input, output, role, every premise, and an input that exists in use.
Gate 2 asks whether it can succeed by construction when everything uncertain goes its way.
A candidate that fails gate 1 leaves the field with the mismatch as its reason. One that
fails gate 2 is repaired on paper or leaves, recorded in `TRIED.md` as "did not work (on
paper)".

A second agent that did not write the cards checks them, under "The checker" in the same
file. A finding it raises stays open until a repair answers it in writing or the framing
dies.

Done when every card in `FIELD.md` carries both gate verdicts and the checker's findings,
each finding answered or the candidate gone.

## 4. Pick the survivor

The candidate at the top of `FIELD.md` that passed both gates is the mechanism to build.
Its cost to test is its **cap**: what this skill may spend building and running its
prototype before the candidate dies. A framing dies only on a framing-level reason: a
candidate's death kills the candidate. The next search after a death starts from a framing
that is not a neighbour of the one that just failed.

Done when one candidate is marked in hand in `FIELD.md` with its cap in money and hours.

## 5. Build the prototype

A **prototype** is the thinnest end-to-end run of the mechanism that clears the **outcome
bar** once: every stage the mechanism needs, each present in the thinnest form that
produces what the next stage reads, on the smallest input the yardstick accepts. Nothing
the outcome bar reads is faked. Everything else is a stub, a fixed input or a hand step,
and each one is written down as a term the ideal path will have to price. Time and money
are not judged here: a prototype that takes an hour on a one-minute bar has still cleared
the outcome bar, and whether its ideal path fits is the study's ceiling test to decide.

Build the stages in the order the unit flows through them, and run the whole path at each
addition, so the first thing that exists is a path and not a stage. A stage that cannot be
made to produce what the next one reads inside the cap is the candidate's death: write the
`TRIED.md` line as "did not work (built)" with the stage and what it produced instead, and
return to step 4. A hand step is allowed in a prototype and forbidden in the mechanism, so
every hand step is named as the stage that would replace it.

Done when one run of the prototype clears the outcome bar on the frozen yardstick, its row
holds the input, the commit and the score, and every stub, fixed input and hand step is
listed in the row.

## 6. Price the ideal path

Draw the mechanism's **ideal path**: one pass per stage, in order, with no retry, no hand
step and no dead time. Give every stage a cost per unit in the purpose's units, and label
each term:

- **measured**, with the ledger row that read it. The prototype's own run supplies most of
  these: the cost of each stage that ran as the mechanism would run it.
- **assumed**, with its source: a rate card, a vendor's figure, a similar stage's reading
  from the ledger, or the cost of the stage that replaces a hand step. An assumed term with
  no source is a guess, and the row says so.

A stub or a fixed input is a stage whose cost the prototype did not pay, so its term is
assumed. A hand step is priced as the stage that replaces it, never at zero. The sum is the
mechanism's ceiling, and the study's ceiling test holds it against the cost bar. Write the
path into the prototype's row as `ideal_path`, one term per stage.

Done when every stage of the mechanism has a term, every term carries its label and its
row or source, and the sum is in the row.

## 7. Hand back

Hand the prototype's row to the ceiling test of [[research-study]]. A pass moves the study
to converge, where [[self-improve]] removes what the mechanism wastes beyond its ideal
path. A miss names the term that sank the mechanism: write the `TRIED.md` line with that
term, mark the candidate dead in `FIELD.md`, and return to step 4 with the next survivor. A
mechanism that misses on an assumed term alone may be re-priced once, when a short probe
can measure that term for less than a tenth of the cap left, and the probe's row carries
the gate above.

The question is answered in the negative only when every surviving framing has died, each
for its own reason, and that sentence, with the reasons, goes back to the study.

Done when `QUESTION.md` carries the state line the ceiling test set, or `TRIED.md` carries a
death line per framing and the study holds the negative.
