# The wide search

The branch of [`explore-then-improve`](../SKILL.md) for a profile with no dominant problem:
the top items are all near their roof, or two reflections in a row found no item whose
reach clears the floor. The problem is then the shape of the system, and a shape is found
by searching a field of readings of the question, not by fixing a stage. The search returns
one candidate shape, and that candidate goes back to step 5 of the skill as the problem in
hand: it gets a cap, a prediction, a fix, an instrument and a verdict like any other.

The search is paperwork, and it is held to the skill's sentence: it earns its time by
killing a candidate more cheaply than a run would. When a short run on things that exist
today would read the fact a card is arguing about, run it and cite the reading. A second
checker round on the same cards with no probe between them is a reflection trigger.

## 1. Frame the question

A **framing** is a reading of the question: what the thing under study does, on which
input, to produce which output, judged how, and what the question's key verb means. It
names no mechanism and no file. Write at least three, each built from its own choice on
every open axis, by the four moves in [framings.md](framings.md): restate the question by
analogy from distant fields, list the axes as fixed or open, draw rows that differ on at
least half of the open axes, and write each row in isolation. Merge two framings that share
input, output and role. Record them in `FRAMINGS.md` in the working directory, in plain
words, each with its status.

## 2. Map the field

Read `TRIED.md` and the project's own history first, then load [[brainstorm]] and
generate before judging. Give every surviving framing at least three candidates, at least
ten in all that differ in mechanism and not in size, at least three absent from `TRIED.md`,
at least two borrowed from another discipline, and one expected to fail with a sentence on
why it might not. The field always holds the two controls under "Controls" in
[measurement.md](measurement.md). For each candidate write its expected gain tied to a
line of the profile, its cost to test, and the result that would kill it. Write the field
into `FIELD.md`, ordered by expected gain over cost to test.

## 3. Gate each candidate on paper

Before any build, write the candidate's card and pass it through the two gates in
[paper-gates.md](paper-gates.md). Gate 1 asks whether it answers the question as posed:
input, output, role, every premise, and an input that exists in use. Gate 2 asks whether it
can succeed by construction when everything uncertain goes its way. A candidate that fails
gate 1 leaves the field with the mismatch as its reason. One that fails gate 2 is repaired
on paper or leaves, recorded in `TRIED.md` as "did not work (on paper)".

A second agent that did not write the cards checks them, under "Checking the cards and
framings" in [second-agent.md](second-agent.md). A finding it raises stays open until a
repair answers it in writing or the framing dies.

## 4. Return the survivor

The candidate at the top of `FIELD.md` that passed both gates is the next problem. Hand it
to step 5 of the skill with its framing, its expected gain as its reach, and its kill
result. A decisive probe on it is registered under "Checking a decisive registration" in
[second-agent.md](second-agent.md) before it launches. A framing dies only on a
framing-level reason: a candidate's death kills the candidate. The question is answered in
the negative only when every surviving framing has died, each for its own reason, and the
next search starts from a framing that is not a neighbour of the one that just failed.
